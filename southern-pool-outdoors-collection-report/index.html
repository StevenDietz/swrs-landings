2 Why the Results Look the Way They Do — Age at Placement

The single largest determinant of recovery is not collection technique. It is how old a balance is on the day it is assigned. Every additional month a consumer balance sits unworked, the debtor is harder to locate, less likely to acknowledge the debt, and more likely to have committed the money elsewhere.

On this portfolio the average account was 279 days past due at assignment. Weighted by dollars — which is the figure that matters, because the largest balances were the oldest — the average was 12.9 months. 71.6% of the placed dollars ($6,903.09 of $9,638.71) were already more than a year past due before Southwest Recovery received them. The oldest was 20.7 months.

First, this is consumer debt, not commercial. Commercial business-to-business recovery generally runs 30–70%. Consumer categories run materially lower — roughly 15–25% for service-type balances. The trade-body aggregate across the entire accounts-receivable-management industry is 11.1% of face value.

Second, the balances are small. The median account here is $254.18 and the average is $507.30. Small-balance consumer accounts are the lowest-yield segment in collections, because the recoverable amount does not support litigation and recovery therefore depends almost entirely on persuading the consumer to pay voluntarily after contact is made.

A realistic expectation for a well-documented consumer service portfolio of this size, placed at 60–90 days past due, is the 15–25% band. This portfolio was placed at a dollar-weighted 12.9 months, which is why it has not reached that band.

5 The Placement-Data Gap

Reaching the right party is where consumer collections succeed or fail. The industry-average right-party-contact rate is approximately 26% — roughly three of every four outbound attempts never reach the person who can resolve the balance. Up to 40–50% of consumer contact data goes stale within 18 months.

7.2  Age at Placement vs. Liquidation Rate

  • An account is roughly 200% (3×) more likely to be collected if assigned at 90 days versus 360 days (Leib Solutions, citing the standard commercial receivables aging curve).
  • Roughly 10–15 percentage points of recovery probability are lost per additional 30 days unworked (MSB Bureau, citing CLLA + ACA International); likelihood of write-off rises ~1% per week past due (Kaplan Group / CLLA).
  • Fresh vs. aged proof: one agency reports a 40% liquidation rate in the first 90 days while a competitor reached only 12–13% on the same account type (AFM). MSB reports 40–60% recovery on B2B accounts placed within 90 days, versus rarely above 10–15% on debts over two years old.
  • "The time for outside, third-party collection agency action is when the debt may still be collectible, best from 90–120 days past due. Waiting too long is to invite a total write-off" (Leib Solutions).

7.4  Impact of Right-Party-Contact & Skip-Trace Data

  • Reaching the right party is the binding constraint — "message quality and payment options are irrelevant if nothing lands" (HL Hunt). Recovery requires a valid contact point → a delivered message → a person who reads it → a payment; the funnel fails most often at step one.
  • The industry-average right-party-contact (RPC) rate is approximately 26%, and roughly 23% of contact centers sit below 20% — meaning about 3 of 4 outbound calls never reach the person who can resolve the debt (DROS.ai; Datacultr). Traditional outbound dialing yields only 4–8% RPC per attempt (Anyreach).
  • Stale/missing data is a leading cause: up to 40–50% of contact data can become outdated within 18 months (NCRi). In one field audit ~18% of numbers were disconnected or wrong (DROS.ai). ACA International member surveys found 62% saw decreased RPCs, 78% experienced call-blocking, 74% had calls mislabeled (TrueAccord).
  • Value of accurate data at placement: better contact enrichment / skip tracing directly lifts RPC and recovery. Skip tracing is "particularly effective for aged debt" and turns otherwise-written-off accounts into recoverable revenue (firstcreditonline.com; commercialcollectors.com).

7.6  Segment Note — Residential Pool & Outdoor Services

  • Residential pool service and outdoor construction balances are consumer debt, not commercial trade debt. That distinction matters: consumer categories liquidate materially lower than B2B, and every contact is governed by the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act and by Texas Finance Code Chapter 392.
  • Balance size is the second constraint. The median account on this file is $254.18 and the average is $507.30. Small-balance consumer accounts are the lowest-yield segment in the industry: the recoverable amount rarely justifies litigation, so recovery depends almost entirely on voluntary payment following contact.
  • Seasonal service relationships compound the contact problem. Pool and outdoor-services customers frequently move, change numbers, or discontinue service in the same season the balance goes unpaid, so the contact data captured at the time of service degrades faster than in trades with recurring year-round billing.
  • What works in this vertical: placing at 60–90 days while the service relationship and its contact record are still current; capturing a signed service agreement or work authorization at the point of sale; and reporting eligible balances to the credit bureaus, which for consumer accounts is often the single strongest motivator to resolve a small balance.

Methodology & caveats

  • Two decay-curve datasets exist and are not identical (the 94.9%→9.3% eight-point CCAA curve vs. the 88.7%→8.9% seven-point CCA of A curve). They are shown separately, not averaged. The 18-month = 15.2% point in the CCA of A series is a published anomaly reproduced faithfully.
  • The classic CCA of A / CCAA / CLLA curve is a long-standing, periodically-updated industry convention; the exact vintage of the published percentages is not always stated. It is the most widely-cited industry standard.
  • Recovery-rate bands are ranges, not guarantees. They vary with documentation quality, debtor solvency, balance size, vertical, and macro conditions. Some fresh-versus-aged figures are self-reported agency benchmarks.
  • Expected collectability and liquidation are two different measures and are not interchangeable. The decay curve estimates the share of a balance still reachable at a given age; liquidation is the share actually collected. Realized liquidation is normally well below expected collectability. Section 4 uses published liquidation rates only.
  • Section 3 uses published industry liquidation rates rather than a Southwest Recovery in-book peer set. A comparison against other SWRS clients in the same trade can be produced on request.
  • This is consumer debt. All activity on this portfolio is conducted under the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (15 U.S.C. § 1692 et seq.) and Texas Finance Code Chapter 392. Where a consumer has invoked a cease-communication right, Southwest Recovery is prohibited by law from further contact, and the account is held rather than worked.
