1. Summary

Portico's relationship with Southwest Recovery Services is ten weeks old at the account level. Four Series entities have placed four former-tenant balances totaling $84,760.22. Nothing has been collected yet. That outcome is consistent with two facts about the portfolio, both of which are shared, fixable levers rather than a verdict on the accounts.

What has happened

All four accounts received the full SWRS front-end: skip-trace and address refresh at intake, Regulation F validation notices, a four-letter demand series, 20 email touches, 14 dialer attempts, credit-bureau reporting, and a live agent conversation on one account. One account is held to credit-reporting only because it arrived outside the limitations period.

What is holding recovery back

The balances were 20 to 47 months past their date of receipt when placed — a dollar-weighted 33 months. The industry collectability curve puts realistic recovery on paper of that age at roughly 10 cents on the dollar, versus ~69 cents at 90 days. Contact data on former tenants decays fastest of any debtor type, and the files arrived without phone, email, or forwarding-address data.

The bigger opportunity

Twelve of the sixteen Series entities have never placed an account. If those entities hold move-out balances, every month they sit unplaced costs roughly 10 to 15 points of recoverable value. A standing placement trigger — tied to the DC 45-day security-deposit accounting — is the single change most likely to move Portico's blended recovery from the low single digits toward the 40–60% band that fresh receivables reach.

Reading the activity honestly

Effort has been consistent and compliant, and every account has verified numbers on file thanks to intake skip-tracing. The constraint is not volume of attempts — it is that former tenants two to four years removed from the lease rarely answer an unknown number, and the credit profiles pulled show no open credit to protect. On files like these, recovery typically comes from the bureau tradeline (a tenant applying for new housing or credit) or a negotiated settlement, not from the demand series alone.

10. Recommendations and decisions for the meeting

For Portico

  1. Adopt a placement trigger for all sixteen Series entities. Place every move-out balance at day 60 after move-out — after the 45-day DC security-deposit accounting closes, while the forwarding address is still current and the balance is fresh. Balances placed inside 90 days sit near the top of the collectability curve.
  2. Sweep the twelve entities that have not yet placed. Any existing move-out balance is losing 10–15 points of recoverable value per month unplaced; the ones under 12 months old are still worth well over half their face value on the curve.
  3. Send the rental application and move-out file with each placement. Phones, email, DOB/SSN, employer, emergency contact, co-signer, and forwarding address. The SWRS portal accepts these as attachments alongside the lease and ledger.
  4. Grant settlement authority on the four current accounts. With no open credit on the tenant profiles pulled, structured settlements are the realistic path; pre-approved authority lets SWRS close when a tenant does engage.
  5. Decide on legal referral for Series D2 before September 30. See section 6.

What SWRS will do

  1. Continue credit-bureau reporting on all four accounts and re-skip-trace at 90 and 180 days on desk.
  2. Move Series 4 and A2 to the settlement-offer track immediately upon authority; re-attempt the Series A2 live-contact number weekly.
  3. Prepare a litigation-viability assessment on Series D2 on request.
  4. Provide a 30-day onboarding checklist and portal walkthrough for the twelve entities that have not placed, and a monthly status report covering all sixteen entities.

Age at placement

  • An account is roughly 200% (3×) more likely to be collected if assigned at 90 days versus 360 days (Leib Solutions, citing the standard commercial receivables aging curve).
  • Roughly 10–15 percentage points of recovery probability are lost per additional 30 days unworked (MSB Bureau, citing CLLA + ACA International); likelihood of write-off rises ~1% per week past due (Kaplan Group / CLLA).
  • Fresh vs. aged proof: one agency reports a 40% liquidation rate in the first 90 days while a competitor reached only 12–13% on the same account type (AFM). MSB reports 40–60% recovery on B2B accounts placed within 90 days, versus rarely above 10–15% on debts over two years old.
  • "The time for outside, third-party collection agency action is when the debt may still be collectible, best from 90–120 days past due. Waiting too long is to invite a total write-off" (Leib Solutions).

Right-party contact

  • Reaching the right party is the binding constraint — "message quality and payment options are irrelevant if nothing lands" (HL Hunt). Recovery requires a valid contact point → a delivered message → a person who reads it → a payment; the funnel fails most often at step one.
  • The industry-average right-party-contact (RPC) rate is approximately 26%, and roughly 23% of contact centers sit below 20% — meaning about 3 of 4 outbound calls never reach the person who can resolve the debt (DROS.ai; Datacultr). Traditional outbound dialing yields only 4–8% RPC per attempt (Anyreach).
  • Stale/missing data is a leading cause: up to 40–50% of contact data can become outdated within 18 months (NCRi). In one field audit ~18% of numbers were disconnected or wrong (DROS.ai). ACA International member surveys found 62% saw decreased RPCs, 78% experienced call-blocking, 74% had calls mislabeled (TrueAccord).
  • Value of accurate data at placement: better contact enrichment / skip tracing directly lifts RPC and recovery. Skip tracing is "particularly effective for aged debt" and turns otherwise-written-off accounts into recoverable revenue (firstcreditonline.com; commercialcollectors.com).

Why balances become uncollectable

Debtors relocate / go darkContact information goes stale — up to 40–50% within 18 months.
Businesses close, dissolve, or file bankruptcy53% of North American firms expected insolvency risk to increase in 2025 (Atradius 2025 Payment Practices Barometer).

Other creditors get paid first"A client who's struggling financially and has only enough money to pay one creditor will likely pay the one who makes the most fuss" (Nolo) — the basis for "the first agency to place wins."
Assets dissipate; time-value erodesEvery uncollected dollar loses present value while collection cost rises; contingency rates climb with age (15–25% fresh, 40–50% at 24 months-plus) (Retrievables).

12. Sources and methodology notes

  • Client records, placements, balances, and work logs are drawn from the SWRS collection system of record as of Sep 14, 2026; balances reconcile to the Account Summary by Manager export of the same date.
  • Age at placement = date placed minus date of receipt on the account. Expected collectability applies the CCA of A seven-point curve with linear interpolation, dollar-weighted by original balance.
  • Peer figures are SWRS's own property-management segment measured on 2022–2023 placements to fixed windows; they are historical outcomes, not predictions. Segment assignments are inferred from client name.
  • Limitations dates are computed from the date of receipt on file under D.C. Code § 12-301 (three years, contract) and must be confirmed against actual breach or last-payment dates. This report is not legal advice.

Caveats

  • Two decay-curve datasets exist and are not identical (the 94.9%→9.3% eight-point CCAA curve vs. the 88.7%→8.9% seven-point CCA of A curve). They are shown separately, not averaged. The 18-month = 15.2% point in the CCA of A series is a published anomaly reproduced faithfully.
  • The classic CCA of A / CCAA / CLLA curve is a long-standing, periodically-updated industry convention; the exact vintage of the published percentages is not always stated. It is the most widely-cited industry standard.
  • Recovery-rate bands (20–30% overall; 30–70% B2B; 40–60% fresh commercial) are ranges, not guarantees, and vary by documentation quality, debtor solvency, balance, vertical, and macro conditions. Some fresh-vs-aged figures are self-reported agency benchmarks.
  • RPC / skip-trace statistics come largely from collections-technology and agency vendors and reflect blended (mostly consumer) portfolios; the underlying principle — stale/missing data kills recovery — applies to both consumer and B2B.
  • Commercial collections operate outside the FDCPA, which governs only consumer debt.
