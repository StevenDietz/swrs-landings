2. Why This Balance Hasn't Collected

This is one debtor relationship, not six separate businesses.

All $16,976.00 placed traces back to a single operation, originally placed as 360 Health Services ($8,488.00) on 12/22/2025. SWRS worked it hard from day one: more than 20 phone contact attempts to five named individuals identified as owners or officers (the listed owner, a CFO, a co-owner, a former CEO, and a warehouse employee), 4 formal collection letters, and validation/workflow emails — all inside the first three months. By March 2026, every direct phone number on file had gone dead or was disclaimed by whoever answered.

The account was reorganized to reach the money through its billing entities instead.

On 03/17/2026, with the direct contact channel exhausted, the collector split the claim into five billing-alias sub-accounts that trace to the same operation — VRC Medical Services, Astor Pharmaceuticals, Revive Med Pharmaceuticals, Integrity Compounding Pharmacy, and IV Nutrition ($8,488.00 combined) — to pursue each one's own accounts-payable contact directly rather than the individuals who'd stopped responding. Since then, SWRS has sent direct email outreach to these entities on an ongoing basis, including six separate emails in the last two weeks alone, most recently yesterday (9/16). No response or payment has come back on any of the five.

Bottom line: this looks like an evasive debtor, not an under-worked file or a legal stop.

The pattern — good-looking contacts who deny association once reached, phone numbers that go dead in bulk, and the same underlying operation billing under at least five different names — is more consistent with a debtor working to avoid collection than with a routine aging problem. It also means there's a live next step: fresh accounts-payable phone numbers are on file for all five billing entities and have not yet actually been dialed — outreach so far has been email-only. Steven can speak to whether SWRS should try that channel, or whether this has crossed the line into a legal referral / skip-trace situation given the pattern.

Separately, placement timing is still worth flagging.

The dollar-weighted average age at placement — from original invoice date to the date SWRS received the file — was 8.9 months. Industry data (Section 4) puts collectability at roughly 88.7% when placed within 30 days, falling to 37–51% by the 6–9 month mark. This particular account may have been headed for a difficult collection regardless of timing, but as a general practice, referring receivables to SWRS sooner after they go past due improves the odds on every future placement, independent of this specific situation.

Source: Commercial Collection Agencies of America, published in the Credit Research Foundation's "Benchmarking the Collectability of Your Accounts Receivable" (Nancy Seiverd).

Corroborating data points from other industry sources:

  • Dun & Bradstreet: an account 90 days past due has a 69.6% chance of being paid; 52.1% at six months; 22.8% at one year.
  • The Kaplan Group / Commercial Law League of America: industry success rate falls to 50% at only seven months past due, with write-off likelihood rising roughly 1% per additional week past due.
  • Right-party-contact (RPC) rate industry-wide averages roughly 26% — meaning a large share of outbound collection contact never reaches the person who can actually authorize payment. This is the most common explanation for high activity with no payment, as with 360 Health Services above.
  • Commercial (B2B) recovery generally runs 30–70% when accounts are fresh and well-documented, versus consumer-adjacent categories like healthcare billing, which typically run lower (15–25%) due to disputes and longer payment cycles — relevant here since all six debtors are healthcare/pharmacy accounts.

Steven can walk through what that would look like against a $16,976 balance.
  • If Go Logistics has any updated contact (a billing manager, a working number, or documentation supporting the charge) that SWRS hasn't tried, sending it over directly would help target the next round of outreach.

6. Sources & Methodology

Account balances, status, dates of referral/assignment, and activity counts are pulled directly from SWRS's internal collection system (ADS) as of September 17, 2026. "Age at placement" is the number of days between the invoice/referral date (DOR) and the date SWRS was assigned the account (DOA). "Expected collectability at placement" applies the CCA of A age-of-debt curve above to each account's age at placement, weighted by dollar amount, and represents a benchmark expectation — not a guarantee or a measure of SWRS's own performance on the file.
