6 Why this book fails — and why most of it is recoverable

The single most important operational insight for this portfolio: most membership default is not a decision. It is a payment mechanism failure. The distinction determines the entire treatment strategy.

Involuntary default — the majority

Expired or reissued debit cards, insufficient funds at month-end, prepaid card exhaustion, bank fraud flags. The pass holder still wants the pass. Published analysis of post-trial subscriptions puts first-payment failure at 66.8% on prepaid cards and 45.4% on debit, versus 21.1% on credit — and prepaid and debit credentials are frequently excluded from card-updater programs, so they cannot be silently refreshed. A value-priced regional park base pays disproportionately by debit and prepaid. This is the recoverable pool, and recovering it reinstates a member rather than closing an account.

Voluntary lapse — the off-season problem

The member stops paying in winter, when the home park is closed and the monthly charge has no visible value. Your own filings note that receivables "are highest in the peak summer months and lowest in the winter months." This segment needs win-back and value messaging timed to the pre-season ramp — not collection pressure. Management has already identified the cost: the Q2 2025 attendance decline reflected "fewer sales of season passes and memberships, and lower renewal rates on season passes."

Recovery is a retention channel. On a subscription book, the collector is often the last human contact a lapsed member has with the brand. Treated as a hostile collection, it ends the relationship and forfeits every future month. Treated as a reinstatement conversation — update the card, restart the pass, keep the guest — it recovers the arrears and the remaining term. Given that management is actively rebuilding the pass base (+6% same-park, season pass sales +7% at Q2 2026), the retained lifetime value is worth more than the arrears.

Channel and language fit

  • Mobile-first, not voice-first. This demographic responds to compliant SMS and email with one-click card update links. Regulation F expressly permits text and email outreach with mandatory opt-out — the modern cadence is both more effective and more defensible than a call program.
  • Bilingual by default. The Sun Belt and Texas parks serve heavily Hispanic markets, and Six Flags already markets in Spanish. Spanish-language recovery is a right-party-contact multiplier, not a nice-to-have.
  • Timing aligned to pay cycles. Retry and outreach scheduled to the 1st and 15th rather than a fixed day-of-month materially changes conversion on a paycheck-to-paycheck base.

8 Brand and compliance — the governor on the whole program

For a consumer entertainment brand, the downside of a recovery program is not a missed dollar. It is a pass holder posting a screenshot. Six Flags has been here before: COVID-era membership billing class actions, a California junk-fee settlement covering roughly 1.4 million people, and a persistent complaint history around cancellation and billing. Any credible partner has to lead with this, not footnote it.

  • Compliance-first by construction. FDCPA and Regulation F governance on every consumer contact — call frequency limits, required disclosures, functioning opt-out on every channel. Commercial accounts worked to the same professional standard even where the FDCPA does not reach them.
  • Auto-renewal law mapping. The federal negative-option landscape is unsettled — the FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025 and re-rulemaking is in motion — while states continue to tighten. We maintain the state-by-state matrix and hold the program to the strictest applicable standard rather than the federal floor.
  • You approve the ladder. Contact cadence, settlement authority, credit-reporting posture and escalation tiers are set by Six Flags per stream, in writing, before anything runs.
  • Reinstatement-first scripting. Every consumer contact leads with restoring the pass. This is a commercial choice as much as a compliance one — it protects the renewal and the brand at the same time.
  • Full auditability. Every contact, promise, dispute and outcome logged and reportable, with complaint rate reported as a first-class KPI beside recovery rate.
