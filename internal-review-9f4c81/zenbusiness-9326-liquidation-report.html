1 Executive Summary

ZenBusiness is SWRS's highest-volume placement relationship: 158,122 accounts totaling $49,516,926 placed across 31 placement months since April 2023. Gross collections to date are $4,133,526, a lifetime liquidation of 8.35%, with $45,402,994 still open.

The single most important structural fact: this portfolio does not liquidate through partial payments — it liquidates through conversions. Only 13,398 of 158,122 accounts (8.47%) have ever paid, but of those that pay, 96.93% of the placed balance is recovered and 12,643 of 13,398 payers (94.36%) paid in full. Average placement is $313.16; average payment is $308.52. Liquidation on this book is therefore almost exactly equal to the right-party contact-and-convert rate. Every point of liquidation improvement has to come from reaching more debtors — not from collecting more per debtor.
  • Cohort performance is stable and predictable. Liquidation matures to a plateau of roughly 9.35% by month 6–9 after placement and does not meaningfully improve afterward (see §5). Cohorts older than 12 months sit at 7.4–9.5%; the 2025 book is the strongest year at 9.16%.
  • Remaining harvest from the current book is limited. Applying the observed maturation curve to every cohort under 12 months old yields $279,438 of additional expected gross collections from placements already in house (§6). Anything above that has to come from a process change, not from waiting.
  • Compliance attrition is the largest single leak. 10,442 accounts ($3,363,067 open) are in Cease and Desist — 6.60% of all accounts, and the rate climbs to roughly 11% as cohorts mature. At the plateau liquidation rate that inventory represents $314,380 of gross collections permanently foreclosed (§7).
  • Placement timing is not the problem here. Median age at placement is 63 days — inside the 60–90 day optimal window — with 92.1% of accounts placed between 31 and 90 days past service date (§8). This client does the hard part right; the constraint is contactability of low-balance consumer/SMB debtors, not staleness.

Of 13,398 paying accounts, 12,643 settled in full for $3,969,869 and only 755 made a partial payment ($163,657 collected, $153,748 still open). There is effectively no payment-plan tail on this book — the account either converts and clears, or it never pays at all.

Implication for revenue planning. Because recovery-per-payer is already at 96.93%, there is no meaningful revenue in negotiating harder or chasing residual balances. Modeled revenue growth must be driven by the conversion rate: at the current average payment of $308.52, each additional 1,000 conversions is worth roughly $308,518 in gross collections.

Reading the curve. Recovery ramps fast — 6.33% in the first quarter, 8.22% by month six — then flattens at a plateau of 9.35% (mean of the 6–9, 9–12 and 12–18 month bands). Practical rule: an account that has not converted within nine months of placement will almost certainly not convert. That is the case for retiring or re-strategizing aged inventory rather than continuing standard-cost effort against it.

Against the industry collectability decay curve (88.7% at 1 month, 68.9% at 3 months, 51.3% at 6 months, 21.4% at 12 months), ZenBusiness places at a median of 63 days past service date — squarely in the 60–90 day optimal window and dramatically fresher than the SWRS book average. Placement staleness is not the constraint on this portfolio.

What that tells us. When a client places this fresh and still liquidates at 8.35%, the binding constraint is the collectability of the underlying obligation and the contactability of the debtor — low-balance ($313.16 average) registered-agent and formation-service fees owed by dissolved or dormant small entities and sole proprietors, many of which never intended to renew. That is a debt-characteristics ceiling, not an effort or timing failure. It also means the decay-curve argument used with most clients does not apply here and should not be used in ZenBusiness-facing material.

12 Findings & Recommended Actions

  1. Confirm the contingency rate and true fee yield. Pull the ZenBusiness fee schedule from ADS and restate §11 with the actual rate, then compare realized fee revenue against cost-to-collect on a $313 average balance. At 158K accounts and 39.5M logged activities, unit economics — not liquidation percentage — is the number that decides whether this relationship is profitable at current pricing.
  2. Attack the cease-and-desist rate, not the aged book. C&D reaches ~11% of a cohort by month 12–18 and represents $314,380 of foreclosed gross collections. Audit contact cadence, channel mix and message content on the first 90 days of a cohort's life — that is where opt-outs are being generated.
  3. Institute a nine-month disposition gate. §5 shows essentially zero incremental recovery after month nine. Define an explicit action at that point — recall to client, alternate channel (litigation screen where balance and entity status support it), or retirement — instead of leaving 56,712 accounts drifting on active desks.
  4. Resolve the 4,298 zero-activity accounts ($1,108,360). These are either load failures or unassigned inventory; both are correctable this week.
  5. Do not use the placement-timing argument with this client. ZenBusiness places at a 63-day median, better than nearly any client in the book. Any client-facing narrative should credit that and locate the constraint where it actually sits — debtor contactability and the dormant-entity profile of the underlying obligations.
  6. Model growth from volume, not rate. With liquidation stable at 7–10% across three years and every balance band, the reliable revenue lever is placement volume, which is already trending up ($2,692,492 in June 2026). Protect and expand the placement pipeline; treat rate improvement as a margin project, not a revenue plan.

13 Methodology & Data Lineage

  • Source of truth. ADS SSRS report “Account Summary – w DOR-DOA,” full export for ClientKey 9326, 158,122 rows. Client identity confirmed via the SWR-API worker (swrs_get_client_info, ClientKey 9326 → ZenBusiness Inc.). ADS is the sole source for placements, balances and payments; Nutshell is not used for financial figures.
  • Reconciliation. Computed totals tie exactly to the SSRS control footer: placed $49,516,926.18, payments $4,133,526.49, balance $45,402,993.69, activities 39,477,855.
  • Liquidation = gross payments ÷ original placed amount, measured on the full book unless explicitly stated as workable-only or net-of-recall.
  • Age at placement = DOA − DOR (date of assignment minus date of record/service). Months on file = report date − DOA ÷ 30.44.
  • Terminal (plateau) liquidation = unweighted mean of realized liquidation in the 6–9, 9–12 and 12–18 month maturity bands = 9.35%. Forward upside applies that rate to cohorts under 12 months on file only; no upside is credited to cohorts already past maturity.
  • Compliance-blocked = Cease and Desist, Deceased, Bankruptcy, Dispute, Attorney, Refuse to Pay, Client Remove, Cancel, Remove, Drop File, Fraud Accnt.
  • Known data caveats. 200 rows share a duplicated client account number ($46,259 placed) — immaterial at 0.09% of the book but excluded from no calculation here; each carries a distinct AGYID. The export carries no payment dates, so recovery timing is inferred from cohort maturation rather than observed directly. One 2024-02 cohort row (single account) is retained as-is.
  • Industry reference values (collectability decay curve, optimal placement window) are drawn from the standing SWRS research index used across all client reports and are not re-derived per report.
