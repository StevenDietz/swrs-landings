The Origin Story

He Didn't Plan to Build a Collection Agency. He Set Out to Recover a Furniture Store's Bad Debt.

Steven Dietz grew up in Texas — a self-described cornfed outdoorsman who worked physically demanding jobs before finding his way into financial services. His first real career role was as a collections manager and credit analyst at a Texas furniture store. He was good at extending credit wisely. And when customers didn't pay, he took it personally. "If you had my furniture, I was going to get it back from you," he has said in multiple interviews. That tenacity was about to change the trajectory of his entire career.

After Aaron's Rents acquired the furniture store, Steven shifted into a pure collections role. He got bored with the standard approach, started digging into the store's P&L, and found a section of written-off bad debt nobody was working. He asked the store manager for permission to take a crack at it. He started recovering money at a rate no one had seen before — so much that the store's performance outpaced every other Aaron's location in the country. The ownership flew in from out of town convinced something was wrong. They brought in an auditor.

"They thought somebody was cooking the books. They did not think it was possible to be booking this much money up and above the historic. They even brought in an auditor, and they found that legitimately we were collecting bad debt — and that was really a key to the store's success."

The owner's son flew back out, met with Steven personally, and offered him a job: travel the country and train the existing collections staff at Aaron's 100+ national locations on what he was doing. He was 20 years old. He had a company credit card and an expense account and thought he was a big deal.

He traveled for two years. Then his wife and he had a child, and the road warrior life stopped working. He told his regional manager he was missing things he couldn't get back — first steps, first words. Aaron's responded by offering him a consulting arrangement that became the cornerstone of something bigger: they handed him 119 of their stores to do outside collections for. In 2004, Steven Dietz founded Southwest Recovery Services. Two desks. A chair. No computers. One phone.

The decades that followed were, in his words, "wildly up and wildly down." He built the company, lost the company, had to file bankruptcy, had friends help him carry furniture out of an office into his garage and start over. He rebuilt. A pivotal executive-level hire — an operations leader whose systematic approach to scoring accounts and coaching collectors transformed the business — drove documented growth cycles of 120%, 140%, and 160% year over year. SWRS expanded to 12 offices across 6 states. Moved into a 6,000-square-foot corporate facility in Addison. Crossed $10M in annual revenue.

Then came the technology layer. Steven deployed Skit.ai voice AI — reporting a 10× ROI on the investment shortly after launch. TCN predictive dialing. Aktos workflow automation. CollectWise legal AI. And finally, Experian's full intelligence infrastructure as the data layer that drives every tool in the stack: PriorityScore, RecoveryScore 2.0, TrueTrace skip tracing, Collection Triggers, and Phone Number ID. The vision he had developed over his collections career — that you must know the account before you work it — became an automated, data-driven architecture operating at national scale.

He still starts every morning at 6 a.m., working bank reconciliations before the staff arrives. At 8 o'clock, he says, it's time to rock.

His Methodology

"Collections is sales in reverse."

Steven's approach — consistent across every interview he has given — is rooted in listening before speaking, self-identifying with the debtor's real concern, and offering a path forward rather than painting people into a corner. "Language and words mean things. If you take the time to listen to what somebody is saying, you can pick up on ways you can help that person get out of what they fear the most."

Leadership Philosophy

Three Beliefs Formed Over a 30-Year Collections Career

Not drafted for a website. Pulled directly from Steven's interviews, his collector training sessions, and the operational decisions he made — first building his collections career in the early 1990s, then building SWRS for 20+ years.

01

Intelligence before contact. Every account. No exceptions.

Steven has held this belief since his first collections job at age 18. "The more information you have at your fingertips, knowledge is power, and it is very, very important to know your adversary before you pick up the phone." He built SWRS around this from day one — scoring accounts, pulling credit data, validating contacts before the first outreach. Today that conviction is operationalized through Experian's full intelligence stack: PriorityScore, RecoveryScore, TrueTrace, and Collection Triggers running on every account before a single call is made.

02

Listen first. Offer solutions. Never paint someone into a corner.

Steven's core collections philosophy — the one he trains every collector on, the one he used to turn a hostile debtor into someone who writes a five-star review — is built on empathy and redirection, not intimidation. "If I can bang my hand on the table and raise my voice, but the hair on the back of your neck is going to stand up, and you will never give me your credit card. However, if I listen to your problem and then offer you solutions, I've offered you hope, and I've offered you a way out." He calls it sales in reverse. It's how SWRS trains every collector and how its AI tools are configured to engage debtors at every touchpoint.

03

You said it. Now do it. Even when it costs you.

When COVID-19 closed the offices in 2020, Steven made a commitment to 60+ employees that they wouldn't go hungry. He stayed up for 72 hours straight — calling every senator, congressman, and state representative in Texas — until he broke through the PPP funding blockade and submitted his application within 18 hours of the program running dry. "I say what I do. I do what I say." That commitment to follow-through, regardless of cost, defines his approach to clients, employees, and every account placed with SWRS.
